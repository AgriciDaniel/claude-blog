# 6-Pillar Dual Optimization

## 6-Pillar Dual Optimization Operating Scope

This hub owns the writing frame for blog posts that must satisfy readers, align with Google quality guidance, and produce passages that can be evaluated for AI answer extraction. It converts the broad strategy in [[Dual Optimization]] into drafting choices for outlines, sections, paragraphs, links, and review gates.

### What This Hub Owns In Article Writing Craft

The six pillars are intent fit, information gain, experience evidence, answer-first structure, source proximity, and final reader satisfaction. The frame is not a claim that Google scores those six items as a formula. It is an editorial operating model built from Google people-first content guidance (`g-helpful-content`), Google's AI features guidance (`g-ai-opt-guide`), the 2025-09-11 Search Quality Rater Guidelines (`g-qrg-full`), and practitioner passage guidance from ZipTie (`ziptie-aio-source-selection`).

### What The Hub Must Not Absorb

This hub must not become a schema manual, a traffic forecast, a CMS workflow, or a generic SEO checklist. Market baselines such as zero-click behavior belong in [[AI Citation Mechanics]] or [[Dual Optimization]], not every writing note. Ranking-update chronology belongs in [[2026 Google Update Timeline]]. If a draft needs rich-result or markup decisions, send it to [[Blog Schema Stack]].

### Spoke Jobs And Deliverable Boundaries

Each spoke owns a narrow pass. [[Internal Link Placement Rules]] decides link timing, not cluster architecture. [[Citation Ready Paragraphs]] shapes claim-bearing prose, not evidence discovery. [[Reader Satisfaction Test]] checks whether the finished draft answers the human task before anyone treats extraction readiness as success.

### Applied Routing Case

A SaaS draft about audit checklists opens with a broad AI-search trend,
then hides the actual checklist under three setup paragraphs.
This hub sends the opening to [[Blog Introduction Patterns]] because
people-first usefulness is the first control (`g-helpful-content`).
It sends the answer block to [[Answer First Section Pattern]] because
Google AI guidance stays tied to visible Search content (`g-ai-opt-guide`).
It sends the proof rows to [[Claim Source Pairing Pattern]] so QRG language
is not treated as a ranking formula (`g-qrg-full`).

### Where This Hub Breaks Down

- A pillar label becomes a score without the rubric owning evidence (`g-helpful-content`).
- Passage polish hides that the draft still lacks original value (`ziptie-aio-source-selection`).
- QRG terminology gets copied into prose as direct ranking mechanics (`g-qrg-full`).
- Internal links get added before the section satisfies its own task (`g-helpful-content`).

### Deliverable Wiring

[[Blog Write Article Contract]] consumes this hub as the article-frame input:
pillar owners, source IDs, blocked spokes, and reviewer handoff notes.
It expects an article package with answer-first structure, nearby evidence,
author or reviewer proof, internal-link rationale, and delivery-gate status (`g-helpful-content`, `g-qrg-full`).
[[Blog Analyzer Score Report]] consumes the same pillar verdicts after drafting
and returns scored findings, owners, and fix states (`g-ai-opt-guide`).

## 6-Pillar Dual Optimization Evidence And Refresh Rules

Use `g-helpful-content` for people-first self-assessment, `g-qrg-full` for quality-evaluator concepts, and `g-ai-opt-guide` for the boundary that Google does not require special AI-only files or markup. Use `ziptie-aio-source-selection` only as medium-confidence practitioner guidance about concise, self-contained passages. Refresh this hub when any assigned source ID changes, when [[Research Pack Index]] records a new Google AI guidance entry, or when the writing rubric starts repeating a tactic that no longer maps to a reader benefit.
